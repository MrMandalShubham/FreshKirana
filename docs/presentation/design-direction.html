Why this exists

The machine is right. The surface isn’t built.

Phases 0–4 made the ordering engine correct — substitutions, weighing, recalls, refunds all behave properly under test. But the storefront was written to prove the API worked, and it shows. Measured on the 375 px phone the spec targets:

Nav is unreachable

The document renders 442 px wide in a 375 px viewport. Two of the five bottom-nav tabs sit off-screen.

Zero imagery

No product photography, no icon set — navigation uses text glyphs. Groceries are bought with the eyes first.

One type size

620 of ~700 text nodes render at 14 px/600. There is no hierarchy to guide the eye anywhere.

The palette

Mandi light

Pulled from a produce market rather than a software company: the deep shade under an awning, turmeric, a fresh sprout, dried chilli, and the warm paper of a shop ledger. Deep green replaces black everywhere — it carries the same weight without the coldness.

Green got brighter

The current #1F7A4D is muddy on an OLED phone in daylight. #1FA45C keeps the same hue and gains the life it was missing — while #14663C handles text where contrast has to hold.

Haldi does the selling

Every screen gets exactly one warm moment — a badge, a saving, a count. Scarcity is what makes it read as energy instead of noise.

Colour still means something

The rule from the old system survives intact: nothing is coloured for decoration, and every coloured state carries a word beside it.

The type

Confident headlines, quiet prices.

Two families. Bricolage Grotesque for anything that has to be felt — it has real character at large sizes and optical sizing built in. Manrope for everything that has to be read, including 40 product names in a row without fatigue.

The motion

Motion that confirms, never decorates.

Every animation here answers a question the shopper is already asking: did that work?, where did it go?, how much longer? Nothing runs longer than 300 ms, everything is transform and opacity only, and all of it stops dead under prefers-reduced-motion.

Add to basket

A dot leaves the button for the basket tab and the count bumps. Answers “did that land?” without a toast stealing the screen.

Loading

Skeletons in the shape of the content, never a spinner. The page never jumps when the data arrives.

The screens

Same information. Completely different feeling.

Nothing below removes a feature that exists today. The veg mark, the legal declarations, the per-kilo price, the five-step timeline all survive — they are simply given room, rhythm and a reason to look.

How it ships

Four parts, in the order that pays off soonest.

Sized to the way the rest of the build runs: one part at a time, each ending green through the same gate. This replaces the single line the plan currently gives the storefront under Phase 8.

Stop the bleeding

The 442 px overflow, the two unreachable nav tabs, and every tap target under 44 px. Pure bug-fixing against rules the codebase already states.

Half a day · ships before anything else

The token layer

Replace the flat palette with the full scale, add the two typefaces with a Devanagari fallback, and set the spacing and radius rhythm. No screen changes — just the vocabulary every later screen draws from.

Foundational · unblocks parts 3 and 4

Home, product and checkout

The three screens that decide whether somebody orders. Hero card, category discs, product rows with imagery, chip-based checkout and the pinned pay bar.

Where the conversion actually moves

Motion and polish

Add-to-basket flight, skeletons, timeline draw, press states, page transitions — plus the product imagery pipeline, which is the one item here that needs real photography rather than code.
