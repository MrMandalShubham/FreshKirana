FreshKirana · Interface design

Four apps,
one shopping trip.

A household, a kirana owner, a rider and an operator each need a different view of the same order. These are the screens each of them sees — drawn from the real colour tokens, real copy and real components in the codebase.

The design system

One green, warm neutrals, and colour that only ever means something.

The palette is deliberately narrow. A grocery screen is already busy with product names, weights, prices and veg marks — so colour is reserved for state: green for what is fine, amber for what needs a decision, red for what has gone wrong. Nothing is coloured for decoration.

Neutrals carry a faint warm cast rather than a cold grey, so a screen full of food photography does not sit on a clinical white.

Type

The phone’s own system font — San Francisco on iPhone, Roboto on Android, with Noto Sans Devanagari for Hindi. It costs nothing to download and renders Indian scripts correctly, which a fashionable web font would not.

Touch

Nothing tappable is under 44 px. The shopper is often one-handed in a kitchen; the picker is standing up holding a crate.

Colour is never alone

Every coloured state also carries a word. A red dot that means “late” is useless to the roughly 1 in 12 Indian men with colour-vision deficiency.

App one Built and running

The shopper

A household doing the monthly shop. The whole design goal is that a 40-item basket takes two minutes, not forty — so the usual basket sits above everything else, before search, before categories.

App two Built and running

The kirana owner

The most important design decision in the whole product: the shop’s app is WhatsApp. A kirana owner will not install and learn a vendor dashboard, so we do not ask them to. Orders arrive in the thread they already read all day, and one tap accepts them. The picking screen is an optional extra for shops with a spare phone at the counter.

App three Design proposal — not built yet

The rider

Held one-handed, outdoors, in sunlight, often in a hurry. So: very few things per screen, very large tap targets, and the cash amount treated as the most important number in the app — a rider who collects the wrong amount creates a problem that costs more than the delivery earned.

App four Design proposal — not built yet

The operator

One or two people running a whole city. This screen is not for browsing orders — it is for finding the handful that are about to go wrong, while there is still time to fix them. Everything healthy stays out of the way.

Sorted by time, not by size

The order with seven minutes left sits above the order worth twice as much. What can still be saved matters more than what is worth most.

Every row has one action

An operator seeing a problem should not then have to work out what to do about it. Call the shop. Refund the item. Assign a rider.

Healthy work is invisible

241 of today’s 248 orders never appear on this screen. A console that lists everything is a console nobody watches.

Honest labelling

Two of these you can open today. Two are drawings.

The shopper app and the kirana flow are running software — the colours, copy and components above were read out of the codebase, not invented for this page. The rider app and the ops console are design proposals for the phases still ahead; they are drawn to the same system so that when they are built, they will look like this.

The screens show sample orders rather than real customers, because there has not been a pilot yet.
